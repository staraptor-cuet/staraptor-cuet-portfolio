## About this file and the 2026 cycle

This file describes the Free Mission category of the TÜBİTAK International and Inter High School UAV Competitions. The rules come from the English 2026 "UAV Competitions Specification", version V7 dated 06.09.2026 [spec p.1]. UAV means unmanned aerial vehicle. TÜBİTAK, the Scientific and Technological Research Council of Türkiye, runs these competitions as part of TEKNOFEST [spec p.9].

The 2026 cycle is finished. The competition week was held at Malatya Tulga Airport on 8–13 September 2026 [spec p.21]. The 2027 specification may change any rule, number or date in this file. The specification also says TÜBİTAK can change it, and that the most recently announced version applies [spec p.78].

Page references in square brackets, such as [spec p.62], use the page numbers printed on the specification pages. For questions, the specification lists the e-mail address uavturkey@tubitak.gov.tr and a Google Groups discussion group for the International UAV Competition, and ihaliseler@tubitak.gov.tr and a separate Google Group for the Inter High School UAV Competition [spec p.81].

## What the Free Mission category is

In 2026 the UAV competitions had three categories: Fixed Wing, Rotary Wing and Free Mission [spec p.10]. In the Fixed Wing and Rotary Wing categories the organisers set the missions. In Free Mission they do not. The specification says that no mission definition is provided and that the teams decide the mission entirely [spec p.61–62]. The team's mission must stay within the safety and compliance limits of the specification [spec p.12].

The mission flown at the competition must be exactly the scenario shown in the team's Preparation and Mission Video. Judges compare it with the mission in the Project Presentation Report and the video, and they do not accept a different mission [spec p.62]. Teams must perform the missions they defined and declared at the report and video stages [spec p.25].

## Which aircraft may enter, and the VTOL rule

The Free Mission category accepts all types of UAV: fixed wing, VTOL, flapping wing, hybrid, rotary wing and others [spec p.12; p.34]. A VTOL (vertical take-off and landing) UAV is defined as a combined aircraft that can take off and land vertically like a helicopter and fly horizontally like a fixed-wing aircraft [spec p.8].

The 2026 specification states three times that VTOL UAVs cannot compete in the Fixed Wing or Rotary Wing categories. They may compete only in Free Mission [spec p.12; p.26; p.34].

In Free Mission the aircraft design does not have to be original. This differs from Fixed Wing and Rotary Wing, where a purchased ready-to-fly UAV or a copied design leads to disqualification. A Free Mission team must clearly declare every ready-made part and every original part. Teams may bring spare parts to the competition area [spec p.35].

## Choosing the mission: what is expected and what is limited

The specification calls it essential to choose and achieve a different and challenging mission. Missions that have been done before, or that are similar to each other, affect a team's success [spec p.62]. Teams may plan their mission to collect as many points as possible under the scoring criteria [spec p.62].

The organisers may reject a mission, or ask for it to be changed, for three reasons: safety, the limits of the flight area, and the limits in the competition rules. The specification gives two examples. A team may be asked to use a paint gun instead of a real weapon mechanism. A team that wants to fly high-speed acrobatics may be given safety restrictions [spec p.62].

The following are forbidden in the competition area [spec p.25]:
- Flights that involve biological, chemical or other hazardous substances that could endanger human health or safety.
- Any flight that could harm people or the environment, including large-scale explosions.

Teams may use replacements such as paint systems or bead guns [spec p.25].

Some missions, such as firefighting, military operations or search and rescue, may be flown as a comparable or demonstration version. The team must get approval from the Advisory and Assessment Committee (AAC), the expert committee appointed by TÜBİTAK, before the competition [spec p.9; p.25].

The team is also fully responsible for procuring all materials and equipment needed for its declared mission [spec p.24].

## Autonomous or manual flight

Free Mission flights may be manual or autonomous, but autonomous missions receive more points [spec p.62]. The specification defines autonomous flight as flight managed by software that makes its own flight decisions without any pilot intervention. It defines manual flight as flight entirely under the pilot's control [spec p.6].

Judges expect the take-off, flight and landing to be autonomous [spec p.69]. A flight controlled entirely by the remote controller gets no points for the "Continuous Autonomous Mission Completion" sub-criterion, which is worth up to 10 points. That sub-criterion loses points for deviations, pauses or any manual intervention during the mission [spec p.68–69].

Two other autonomy sub-criteria add up to 5 more points [spec p.68–69]:
- Mission Planning Autonomy (up to 3 points): the aircraft chooses its route, selects targets, makes alternative choices and runs decision algorithms without human commands.
- Environmental Perception and Adaptation (up to 2 points): the aircraft senses its surroundings with sensors and changes its route or mission in response. Examples include wind, obstacles and losing a target.

## Safety equipment and fail-safe rules

These rules apply to every category, including Free Mission.

- **Radio fail-safe.** A fail-safe is a system that brings the aircraft to a safe landing in an emergency. A clearly defined radio fail-safe mode is mandatory, and a UAV that fails the fail-safe check may not fly [spec p.32]. The UAV must switch to fail-safe within 5 seconds of losing the radio signal [spec p.35]. Judges test this on the ground by switching off the transmitter [spec p.33].
- **VTOL fail-safe.** When the fail-safe activates, a VTOL UAV must switch to rotary-wing mode and make a controlled descent at half throttle. If the judge commands it, the throttle is cut [spec p.34]. If a required function is missing, the technical judge must still be convinced that the UAV can perform the fail-safe. Otherwise the UAV may not fly [spec p.34].
- **Radio link.** The radio control system must have end-to-end encryption [spec p.35]. Any electromagnetic broadcast, jamming, spoofing or frequency interference that affects another competitor leads to disqualification [spec p.35]. The 2026 specification makes encrypted telemetry mandatory only for Fixed Wing and Rotary Wing [spec p.36].
- **Power cut-off.** The UAV needs an easily reachable power on/off switch that does not rely on an external circuit breaker. The switch must cut power within 2 seconds, and teams demonstrate this at technical control. The video must explain how the switch is built into the aircraft. Powering the UAV without the flight judges' approval is grounds for disqualification [spec p.35].
- **Arming.** Motors must be armed and disarmed with stick movements on the remote controller [spec p.32].
- **Judge termination.** If flight judges see a safety risk, they can ask for the flight to be ended. The UAV must then switch to fail-safe mode and land safely [spec p.22].

## Technical control and flight safety inspection

Every team must pass a pre-flight technical control [spec p.32]. Expert judges examine the UAV's weight components, structural strength, mechanical movement and the safety of its electronics [spec p.22]. Teams must use the UAV described in their report and video. They bring it with the propellers removed and with every component and connection mechanism [spec p.32]. They must also bring any spare batteries of the same capacity, model and weight. Approved batteries may be swapped or charged in the competition area [spec p.32].

The flight safety check looks especially at the following [spec p.33]:
- whether the UAV matches the technical drawings in the report;
- structural, electrical, mechanical and propulsion reliability;
- whether all parts are securely mounted;
- connections secured with safety wire, liquid adhesive or lock nuts;
- propeller integrity and whether each motor turns in the correct direction;
- wires and connectors that are thick enough;
- enough radio range;
- end-to-end encryption;
- automatic fail-safe on signal loss.

Free Mission teams that declared components for the "Economy & Simplicity", "Innovation & Originality" or "Domestic Product Usage" criteria must have those parts mounted on the UAV at technical control [spec p.24]. A UAV that was damaged or crashed needs a detailed technical control before it flies again [spec p.36].

## Pilots and people in the flight area

- The team pilot must be one of the student members. Advisors and Advisor Teachers cannot be pilots [spec p.23].
- At technical control, the pilot must provide a pre-flight document. Pilots whose documents are accepted get a marked name tag, and pilots without a marked tag may not enter the flight area [spec p.23].
- A Free Mission mission that needs more than one pilot requires a UAV pilot licence for each pilot. One pilot may not control several UAVs at the same time, so each UAV needs its own pilot [spec p.36].
- Helmets provided by TÜBİTAK must be worn during the flight [spec p.36].
- Advisors and Advisor Teachers may not enter the technical control tent, the interview tent or the flight area [spec p.22].
- TÜBİTAK may place a Turkish Aeronautical Association (THK) pilot in the area for safety. This does not give teams a right to piloting help [spec p.23].
- During the competition week, no team member may act as a pilot for another team [spec p.13].

## Flight slots, timing and number of attempts

Before the competition week, the AAC sets the Free Mission groups and the flight order based on each team's mission. Teams cannot object to the grouping or the order [spec p.23]. Each team also gets fixed time slots for technical control and the interview [spec p.24].

Free Mission flights are held in at most 3 rounds, with one flight right per round. Teams do not have to use all three [spec p.24]. Page 24 says that for a team using all three rights, only its highest-scoring flight counts. Page 75 describes the ranking differently: the organisers rank and announce results after rounds 1 and 2, and after round 3 they use the higher of the totals from the second and third flight rights. The specification does not explain how these two statements fit together.

Timing rules [spec p.25]:
- Teams cannot postpone their flight. A team that is not ready, or does not start, when its turn comes loses that flight right.
- The flight must start within 5 minutes of the judge's permission. A team that has not prepared in advance, for example by entering coordinates beforehand, gets a warning and a deduction under "Team Awareness".
- A team that has not taken off within 8 minutes gets a second warning and loses that flight right.
- A failed take-off attempt may be retried within the same 8 minutes.

To score, the UAV must complete the take-off, flight and landing. A UAV that does not stay airborne, or crashes before stable flight, is unsuccessful, and teams cannot object. This does not apply when bad weather is the cause. After repairs, a team re-enters technical control and waits for the next available slot [spec p.25].

Dates: [spec p.9; p.21].

**Project Presentation Report** [spec p.14–16]
- Covers the team, the UAV, the mission and the budget.
- Written on the official template, in Turkish or English.
- At most 17 pages, counting the TEKNOFEST covers and the annexes.
- Scored as the average of three judges' scores. Teams below the passing score are eliminated.

**Preparation and Mission Video** [spec p.16–19]
- At most 20 minutes long, HD, MP4 format, with no music, in Turkish or English. It is uploaded publicly and its link is added to the system.
- The flight section is at most 10 minutes for Free Mission. It shows the full mission, including take-off and landing, without any cuts, even when several vehicles fly.
- The screen is split three ways: the aircraft, a wide shot of the pilot, and a direct screen recording of the ground station.
- If the UAV cannot fly or crashes in the video, the team gets zero points and is eliminated.
- The briefing section is at most 10 minutes. It explains the mission and why the team chose it.
- Small design changes are allowed if they do not change the mission scenario. The video must explain them.

**At the competition** [spec p.24]: technical control, a 15-minute question-and-answer interview with the judges, then the flights.

**Plagiarism and AI:** Copied material, or the use of artificial intelligence (AI), in the report or the video leads to elimination without the right to object [spec p.16; p.19–20].

**Objections:** Teams scoring at most 25 points below the passing score may object within 3 days, through the application system only [spec p.16; p.20].

## Free Mission scoring: the 100-point table

The same table applies to the International competition and the Inter High School competition [spec p.68–69].

| Criterion (maximum points) | Sub-criteria with points |
|---|---|
| 1 Autonomy (15) | Mission planning autonomy 0–3; Environmental perception and adaptation 0–2; Continuous autonomous mission completion 0–10 |
| 2 Stability (10) | Take-off 0–3; Cruise 0–4; Landing 0–3 |
| 3 Capability (25) | Mission type difficulty coefficient 0–10; Mission success degree 0–5; Mission performance efficiency (time, accuracy, energy) 0–5; Adaptation to changing conditions (wind, signal, obstacles) 0–5 |
| 4 Mission Effectiveness & Demo Quality (10) | Mission story consistency 0–3; Demonstration clarity 0–3; User impact and applicability 0–4 |
| 5 Domestic Product Usage (10) | Software 0–5; Mechanical/electronic 0–5 |
| 6 Innovation & Originality (15) | Original mission scenario 0–5; Technological innovation 0–5; Scientific/industrial contribution 0–5 |
| 7 Economy & Simplicity (10) | Unnecessary hardware 0–5; Cost/performance 0–5 |
| 8 Team Awareness (5) | Teamwork / crew resource management 0–2; Time management 0–1; Compliance with rules 0–2 |

**How judges score:** At least three judges score each criterion, and the team's total is the sum of the criterion averages [spec p.74]. A judge who works for the same institution as a team member hands that team's scoring to another judge [spec p.75].

**Worked example from the specification:** The scoring example gives these criterion averages: 12, 7, 20, 8, 4, 10, 6 and 3. They add up to 70 points [spec p.75].

## What the judges look for in each criterion

- **Stability:** loss of control on take-off, wobbling or oscillation during cruise, and hard or uncontrolled landings. As an example, a rotary-wing UAV must hover for at least 10 seconds and a fixed-wing UAV must fly straight and stable for at least 10 seconds [spec p.70].
- **Capability:** the judges set the difficulty coefficient in advance, and more complex missions get a higher coefficient. Mission success degree is the share of mission targets and steps that were completed [spec p.70].
- **Mission Effectiveness & Demo Quality:** a clear explanation of "what is the problem, what is the solution, why this way", the match between the report and the flight, and how useful the mission is in real life [spec p.71].
- **Domestic Product Usage:** only parts and software developed or produced in the team's own country count. Buying a foreign product from a local seller does not count. Filament, screws, foam adhesive and foreign autopilots sold by local companies are given as examples that do not count [spec p.71–72].
- **Innovation & Originality:** new sensors, new algorithms, special mechanical designs, the team's own software or controller, new energy sources, new application fields, original auxiliary equipment, and publication or patent potential. A feature counts only if it has not been declared in another competition. The team must document and prove its claims, especially software claims [spec p.69; p.72–73].
- **Economy & Simplicity:** "less but functional equipment", and high success for an affordable cost [spec p.73].
- **Interview evidence:** In the interview, claims need concrete, verifiable evidence, including domestic contribution rates. A part that was declared but is not on the UAV earns no points [spec p.24].

## Penalties and reasons for elimination

- **Team Awareness deduction.** In Free Mission, unsportsmanlike behaviour or behaviour against team awareness costs 0–5 points from the Team Awareness criterion [spec p.63]. Teams that keep behaving badly after warnings may be eliminated or removed from the area [spec p.63].
- **Elimination.** The specification lists these reasons for elimination [spec p.63]:
  - an aircraft that does not comply with the specification;
  - running motors or attempting flights in unauthorised areas;
  - hardware or software changes made without the approval of the AAC or the judges;
  - sabotaging other teams;
  - false or misleading declarations in team information, licences, permits or official documents.
- **Other disqualifications.** These also lead to elimination or disqualification:
  - jamming or spoofing [spec p.35];
  - powering the UAV without the judges' approval [spec p.35];
  - verbal or physical interference with officials [spec p.23];
  - a first-degree family relationship or conflict of interest between students and the Advisor [spec p.12–13; p.79].
- **Aggressive behaviour.** Aggressive or rude behaviour, including online, can lead to expulsion or a ban of at least 1 year [spec p.79].
- **Security breaches.** The AAC may apply sanctions for these behaviours [spec p.80–81]:
  - flying an untested vehicle for the first time at the competition;
  - spinning propellers indoors, for example inside a tent;
  - handling a crashed UAV while its propellers are still turning, unless it is very urgent;
  - not intervening in time when the UAV leaves the boundary and the judge orders a landing;
  - flying again after a crash without a new technical control.

## Team composition rules

- Each team has 5 to 10 members, counting the Team Responsible and the Advisor, if there is one [spec p.12].
- **International UAV Competition:** associate, undergraduate, graduate and doctoral students from any country. The Team Responsible must be a university student. One Advisor is optional. An Advisor is an academic or a UAV expert [spec p.12–13].
- **Mixed teams:** high school students may join a university team, but they must be fewer than half of all members, counting the Advisor. For example, a team of 10 may have at most 4 high school students [spec p.12].
- **Inter High School UAV Competition:** high school students. The Team Responsible is an Advisor Teacher [spec p.12–13].
- All members except the Advisor and the Advisor Teacher must be students on the application date [spec p.13].
- A person may be in only one team, and may enter only one of the two TÜBİTAK UAV competitions [spec p.13].
- The team name may be at most 20 characters [spec p.14].
- Applications are made online at www.t3kys.com/en [spec p.14].
- Members under 18 on 10 July 2026 needed a Letter of Consent from a parent or guardian [spec p.15; p.21].
- International teams must take their UAVs apart for Turkish customs [spec p.22]. Delays at customs or in cargo are not accepted as excuses [spec p.79].

## Financial support, refunds and ethics clauses

**Project support** [spec p.20–21]
- Covers materials and travel, up to a combined maximum of 120,000 TRY.
- The team requests it with a budget table in the report. TÜBİTAK and the AAC evaluate the budget.
- It is paid in cash to the Team Responsible at the competition area, and only if the team passed every stage and became a finalist.
- The Team Responsible must sign a Letter of Commitment at registration.
- Teams that passed but did not receive support get no separate travel support. They may still continue with their own means [spec p.14–15; p.21].
- Teams may also cooperate with public and private sector organisations [spec p.21].

**Refunds** [spec p.78]: TÜBİTAK may ask for a full refund of the support if a team does any of the following:
- skips technical control or does not fly;
- misses the award ceremony after winning an award;
- is expelled for ethical violations, technical inadequacy or unsportsmanlike behaviour;
- submits unrealistic or misleading documents.

**Payments to teams from abroad** [spec p.78]: Teams from abroad are paid in cash at the competition area. If cash payment is not possible, they must give a bank account that accepts international euro or dollar transfers.

**Ethics** [spec p.6; p.62–63]
- An ethical violation includes copying from another team's report or video.
- Statements or actions meant to influence or mislead judges are forbidden.

**Publicity:** UAVs authorised by TÜBİTAK and TEKNOFEST to fly only cameras may film at any time during the competition [spec p.22].

## Checklist: what a VTOL team entering Free Mission had to prepare (2026 rules)

This list only repeats rules from the sections above.

- Enter Free Mission. Under the 2026 rules, it was the only category open to VTOL UAVs [spec p.34].
- Define your own original and challenging mission. Get AAC approval for any demonstration version, and use no hazardous substances [spec p.25; p.62].
- Keep the total take-off weight at 25 kg or less, the flight at 15 minutes or less, and the altitude at 120 m or less [spec p.23; p.34; p.62].
- Set the fail-safe to switch to rotary-wing mode, descend at half throttle and cut the throttle when the judge commands. It must trigger within 5 seconds of losing the signal [spec p.34–35].
- Use end-to-end encrypted radio control, arm the motors with the sticks, and fit a power switch that cuts power within 2 seconds [spec p.32; p.35].
- Submit a report of at most 17 pages, and a video of at most 20 minutes with an uncut flight and the three-way screen layout [spec p.16–18].
- Declare every ready-made and original part, and bring evidence for the interview [spec p.24; p.35].
- Use a student pilot with an accepted pre-flight document, and a licensed pilot for each UAV if several fly [spec p.23; p.36].
- Fly autonomously from take-off to landing to collect the most autonomy points [spec p.69].
