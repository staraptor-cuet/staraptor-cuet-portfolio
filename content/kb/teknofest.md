## What TEKNOFEST is and who organises it

TEKNOFEST's official site calls TEKNOFEST "the world's largest aerospace and technology festival". The TEKNOFEST home page does not name a single organiser. It lists many stakeholders, including the Ministry of National Defence, the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure, TÜBİTAK, Baykar, ASELSAN and Turkish Aerospace Industries.

The Turkish Technology Team Foundation (T3 Vakfı) lists TEKNOFEST among its brands. The foundation describes TEKNOFEST as a festival "organized in partnership with numerous institutions". In its introduction to its brands, the foundation says it works to bring a passion for technology to everyone "from 7 to 70".

Different bodies run individual TEKNOFEST competitions. TÜBİTAK runs the International UAV Competition through the Department of Competitions and Organization in the TÜBİTAK Directorate for Science and Society, within the scope of TEKNOFEST [spec p.9].

## International UAV Competition: organiser, purpose and categories

TÜBİTAK organises the International UAV Competition within the scope of TEKNOFEST. A TÜBİTAK-assigned Advisory and Assessment Committee (AAC) creates, applies and supervises the technical rules [spec p.9]. The competition aims to direct students toward UAV technologies and to create a meeting point in Türkiye for national and international students [spec p.9]. A separate Inter High School UAV Competition shares the same specification.

In 2026 the International UAV Competition had three categories [spec p.10-12]:

- **Fixed Wing.** Mission 1 tests autonomous flight, the volume of a cylindrical storage box, how fast the aircraft goes from box to ready-to-fly, low weight, flight speed, stable flight and manoeuvrability. Mission 2 adds payload capacity and accurate drops. The aircraft must find the targets by image processing. It drops a red payload on a 4 m x 4 m blue target and a blue payload on a 2 m x 2 m red target [spec p.10].
- **Rotary Wing.** The missions are similar, but the storage box is a rectangular or square prism. In Mission 2 the aircraft drops a red payload on a blue regular hexagon with 2 m sides and a blue payload on a red equilateral triangle with 1 m sides, found by image processing. An optional Mission 3 is to pick up the first dropped payload and drop it on the other target [spec p.10-11].
- **Free Mission.** The team chooses its own mission within the specification's safety and compliance limits. Any UAV type may enter [spec p.12].

## Who can apply to the International UAV Competition (2026 rules)

The 2026 specification sets these application rules for the International UAV Competition:

- National and international associate, undergraduate and graduate students may apply as a team. Doctoral students may also apply [spec p.12].
- Each team needs one Team Responsible, who must be a university student. The Team Responsible handles the application, report and video submission, and coordination [spec p.13].
- A team may have one Advisor. The Advisor is an academic or an expert with UAV knowledge and experience, and is optional. An Advisor must be registered at application [spec p.13].
- A team has at least 5 and at most 10 members, counting the Team Responsible and any Advisor [spec p.12].
- Mixed teams with high school students are allowed. High school students must be fewer than half of all members, Advisor included. For example, a team of 10 may have at most 4 [spec p.12].
- Every member except the Advisor must be a student on the application date [spec p.13].
- A person may be in only one team, and in only one of TÜBİTAK's two UAV competitions [spec p.13].
- Students must not be first-degree relatives of the Advisor or have a conflict of interest with the Advisor [spec p.12-13].
- Members under 18 on 10 July 2026 needed a consent letter signed by a parent or legal guardian [spec p.15].

## Aircraft and safety rules in the International UAV Competition (2026)

The 2026 specification sets these aircraft rules for the International UAV Competition:

| Rule | Fixed Wing | Rotary Wing | Free Mission |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum take-off weight | 4 kg | 4 kg | 25 kg [spec p.34] |
| Aircraft type allowed | Fixed wing only | Rotary wing only | Any type [spec p.34] |
| Original design required | Yes. Bought ready-to-fly or replica aircraft are disqualified. | Yes | No, but ready-made parts must be declared [spec p.35] |

Rules for all categories:

- A radio fail-safe mode is mandatory. A UAV that fails the fail-safe check may not fly [spec p.32].
- The UAV must switch to fail-safe within 5 s of losing its radio signal [spec p.35].
- Fail-safe settings [spec p.33-34]:
  - Fixed wing: throttle cut, full up elevator ("altitude" in the specification's English text), full right rudder, full right aileron, flaps full down.
  - Rotary wing: half throttle with controlled descent; throttle cut on the judge's command. Return-to-launch is not allowed.
  - VTOL: switch to rotary-wing mode, then half throttle with controlled descent; throttle cut on the judge's command.
- The radio control system must have end-to-end encryption. Fixed Wing and Rotary Wing teams must also use end-to-end encrypted telemetry [spec p.35-36].
- Teams that jam, spoof or interfere with frequencies are disqualified [spec p.35].
- An on-board power switch must cut power within 2 s [spec p.35].
- Motors are armed and disarmed with stick movements [spec p.32].
- Judges may suspend the competition in strong wind. The wind speed threshold is 25 km/h [spec p.22].

## Category for a VTOL or hybrid aircraft in the International UAV Competition

Under the 2026 specification, a VTOL aircraft cannot enter the Fixed Wing or Rotary Wing category of the International UAV Competition. The specification states: "VTOL UAVs can't compete in the Fixed Wing or Rotary Wing categories, they can only compete in the Free Mission category" [spec p.12]. Sections 8.3.1 and 9.3 repeat this rule. Section 9.3 also states that only fixed wing UAVs may compete in the Fixed Wing category [spec p.26, p.34].

The Free Mission category accepts all UAV types, including fixed wing, VTOL, flapping wing, hybrid and rotary wing [spec p.12, p.34]. Free Mission rules that matter for a VTOL team:

- The take-off weight limit is 25 kg [spec p.34].
- Flight time must not exceed 15 minutes. Before the flight, the team gives the judges a briefing of at most 5 minutes [spec p.62].
- Flights may be manual or autonomous. Autonomous missions receive higher points [spec p.62].
- The aircraft must fly the same mission shown in the report and the video [spec p.62].
- If a mission needs more than one pilot, each pilot needs a UAV pilot license, and each UAV must have its own pilot [spec p.36-37].

## Stages, deliverables and the 2026 calendar (past cycle)

The International UAV Competition has two evaluation stages before the competition week [spec p.14-20]:

- **Project Presentation Report.** Teams upload it at www.t3kys.com/en, in Turkish or English. Reports over 17 pages are not accepted. Three judges score it. Plagiarism or AI use leads to elimination [spec p.14-16].
- **Preparation and Mission Video.** The video may be up to 20 minutes, in HD MP4 with no music. It must show the same mission as in the competition, flown without cuts. A split screen shows the aircraft, the pilot and the ground station. Fixed Wing and Rotary Wing videos must show a figure-eight manoeuvre and a landing without a crash [spec p.16-19].

## Scoring in the International UAV Competition (2026)

The 2026 specification scores the International UAV Competition categories as follows.

**Fixed Wing and Rotary Wing** [spec p.64]:

| Part | Fixed Wing | Rotary Wing |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation and Mission Video | 10 | 10 |
| Mission 1 | 30 | 30 |
| Mission 2 | 70 | 80 |
| Total | 110 | 120 |

Mission scores compare each team with the best team on several measures: storage box volume, preparation time, empty weight, payload weight, drop distance from the target centre and flight time. An autonomy coefficient c multiplies the mission score. It is 1.0 for autonomous take-off, flight and landing, and 0.7 when take-off and/or landing is manual [spec p.64-65]. For example, the Mission 1 formula weights are 6 (volume) + 6 (preparation time) + 6 (empty weight) + 12 (flight time) = 30 points. Judges can deduct 0-5 points for unsportsmanlike conduct [spec p.63]. A team that fails Mission 2 cannot win a ranking award [spec p.76].

**Free Mission.** Judges score eight main criteria, with a total of 100 points [spec p.68-69]:

- autonomy (up to 15)
- stability (up to 10)
- capability, including a 0-10 mission difficulty coefficient (up to 25)
- mission effectiveness and demonstration quality (up to 10)
- domestic product usage (up to 10)
- innovation and originality (up to 15)
- economy and simplicity (up to 10)
- team awareness (up to 5)

## Prizes and support in the International UAV Competition (2026)

The 2026 International UAV Competition listed these awards for each of its three categories [spec p.76-77]:

| Award | Team | Advisor |
|---|---|---|
| 1st place | 250,000 TRY | 20,000 TRY |
| 2nd place | 200,000 TRY | 15,000 TRY |
| 3rd place | 175,000 TRY | 12,000 TRY |
| TUSAŞ Special Award (1st place teams) | 100,000 TL | none |
| Performance award (55 points and above) | points x 750 TRY | shared with the team |

Ranking awards and the TUSAŞ Special Award are split equally among the team members, excluding the Advisor. Performance awards go only to finalist teams that took no TÜBİTAK financial support and won no ranking award. They are split equally among all members, including the Advisor [spec p.7, p.76-77].

**Project support** [spec p.20-21]:

- TÜBİTAK may give project support for materials plus transport to the competition city. The upper limit is 120,000 TRY in total.
- The support is paid in cash in the competition area, only to teams that become finalists.
- Teams that passed but are not entitled to support get no separate transport support.
- Teams from abroad receive payments in cash. If cash is not possible, they must give a bank account open to international Euro or Dollar transfers [spec p.78].

**Other support.** The competition page also lists digital certificates, technical tours, internships and the TEKNOFEST Startup Program. TÜBİTAK can ask for the project support to be paid back, for example if the team does not fly [spec p.78].

## Other TEKNOFEST UAV competitions

The TEKNOFEST competitions list includes other UAV competitions besides the International Unmanned Aerial Vehicle Competition. The list includes:

- Inter-High School Unmanned Aerial Vehicles Competition
- Swarm UAV Competition
- Fighter UAV Competition
- Fighter UAV Stars Competition
- Fighter UAV Hunter Drone Competition

The list shows no competition with "VTOL" in its name.

- **Fighter UAV Competition (2026).** The competition aims to create air-to-air and air-to-ground fighter scenarios between UAVs. Teams have 3-15 members. The stages were a technical qualification form, a critical design report, a system identification video with flight-pilot evidence video, and a final. The final was at Siirt Airport on 24-30 August 2026. Prizes were 600,000 / 500,000 / 400,000 ₺.
- **Swarm UAV Competition (2026).** Teams develop software and algorithms for UAVs that complete tasks as a swarm. Students and graduates of universities in Türkiye or abroad may enter. Teams have 3-10 members. The final was in Gaziantep on 7-11 September 2026. Prizes were 300,000 / 250,000 / 200,000 ₺.

Both 2026 events are past.

## Practical notes for international teams in the International UAV Competition

The 2026 specification and the competition page give these points for teams from outside Türkiye:

- Applications, reports and video links go only through the online system at www.t3kys.com/en. No printed documents are requested at application [spec p.14].
- The report and the video may be in Turkish or English [spec p.15, p.19].
- International teams must disassemble their UAVs to avoid being held at Turkish customs [spec p.22].
- Customs or cargo delays are not accepted as an excuse for not attending [spec p.79].
- Questions can go to uavturkey@tubitak.gov.tr or to the Google Group "uluslararasi-iha-yarismasi" [spec p.81].
- The competition page lists the National Technology Teams Support Program, led by the Turkish Technology Team Foundation, which offers international competition guidance, material support and transport and accommodation assistance.
- The TEKNOFEST UAV training videos on the competition page are in Turkish.

No visa or invitation letter process is described in the specification or on the pages checked.
