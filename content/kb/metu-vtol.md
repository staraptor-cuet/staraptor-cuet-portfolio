## What the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition is and who organises it

The METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition is held at Middle East Technical University (METU) in Türkiye. University engineering teams design, build and fly vertical take-off and landing (VTOL) aircraft. The competition website brands the 2025 edition as "VTOL'25".

The METU Aerospace Engineering Department organises the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition. Dr. Ozan Tekinalp leads it. The organising committee listed on the competition contact page is:

- Prof. Dr. Ozan Tekinalp, Middle East Technical University
- Asst. Prof. Dr. Egemen Gorkem Guloglu, Middle East Technical University
- Dr. Metehan Yayla, Flight Dynamics and Control Engineer
- Alp Tikenogullari, Aerodynamics Engineer
- Abdurrahim Muratoglu, Graduate Research Assistant
- Onur Sevindik, Aerospace Engineer

The home page describes the 2025 scenario as a simulated heavy cargo delivery to an offshore platform. The scenario has three parts: a reconnaissance flight as far as possible with no payload, a heavy-payload flight with a precise landing, and a slung-load drop on a target. The 2025 rules page defines the scored missions under different names: Ferry Flight, Pirouette, and Fixed-Wing Loiter Performance.

The official language of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition is English.

## Eligibility and team rules of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition (2025 rules)

The 2025 (9th edition) METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition set these team conditions:

- Entry is open to engineering students from universities.
- Each team needs an academic advisor from its own institution.
- At least 3/4 of team members must be undergraduate students.
- Two or more universities may form one joint team.
- Each institution may enter at most 2 teams. The technical committee may accept more teams from one university if it has 3 or more outstanding applications.
- Proposals and final design reports must be in English.
- Ready-to-fly kits are forbidden. Using one leads to disqualification.
- Each aircraft must be registered in the Turkish Civil Aviation Authority (SHGM) database before flight.
- The pilot must be an RPV pilot registered with the Turkish Civil Aviation Authority. The organisers can provide a pilot on an as-available basis if told in advance. Teams may register more than one pilot if each has SHGM pilot registration.

Budget and support:

- Teams may be reimbursed up to 90,000 TRY for manufacturing costs. Valid invoices must be handed in at the competition. The home page says this applies to invited teams from Turkish universities.
- Manufacturing costs of international teams are not reimbursed.
- Travel costs are covered separately and accommodation is reserved. The home page says travel is reimbursed for Turkish teams that submit the final report and a flight video and attend with their aircraft.
- Members of winning teams receive special school supplies.

## Aircraft requirements of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition (2025 rules)

The 2025 rules of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition set these aircraft requirements:

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Configuration | Takes off and lands vertically, then transitions to forward flight. Lift in forward flight comes mainly from the wings. Propeller-lifted and propeller-driven. Multirotors and helicopters are not allowed. |
| Take-off weight | Below 20 kg including payload. Weighed before take-off. |
| Propulsion | Brushless electric motors, ESCs and batteries only. No external take-off assistance. All energy comes from onboard batteries. |
| Autonomy | All missions are flown autonomously. |
| Altitude | At least 20 m above ground level in forward flight. Vertical phases may go lower. The committee may change this limit. |
| Flight pattern | Rounded rectangle, 300 m × 100 m. Leaving the safety zone makes the flight invalid. |
| Battery | Battery capacity must be the same in all missions. |
| Modular changes | Wing, tail and propellers may be changed between missions. Teams must show the change can be done within 15 minutes. |
| Units | Scoring uses kg and seconds. |

Other flight rules:

- The aircraft must take off within 10 minutes of the referee's start signal.
- Missions are flown in order. A mission cannot start until the previous mission has a successful score.
- A mission is scored only after a successful landing.
- Damage counts as repairable if the aircraft can be repaired and operated within 5 minutes. The team must then show a demonstration flight.
- An identical backup aircraft may replace a damaged one.
- Teams hand in flight logs after each successful flight.
- Teams may fly a mission more than once. The highest score counts.
- Flight order follows the report score, highest first.

## Mission 1 of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition: Ferry Flight (2025 rules)

Objective: in Mission 1 (Ferry Flight) of the 2025 METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition, the aircraft flies as many laps of the flight pattern as possible in 5 minutes. It carries no payload.

Procedure:

- Timing starts when the aircraft passes Checkpoint A.
- A lap counts when the aircraft flies the full pattern and passes Checkpoint A again, and the referee raises a green flag.
- Only whole laps count.
- The aircraft lands on a 3 m × 3 m QR-code landing area.

Score:

M1 = (1 / OEW1) × (N_lap / N_lap,max + K_prec)

- N_lap: laps completed by the team.
- N_lap,max: most laps completed by any team.
- K_prec: landing precision factor. It is 1 if every ground contact point is inside the QR-code area, and 0 otherwise.
- OEW1: operating empty weight measured at the start of Mission 1, in kg.

What is scored in Mission 1: laps compared with the best team, landing precision, and a low operating empty weight.

Source: https://vtol-ae.metu.edu.tr/en/rules.html

## Mission 2 of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition: Pirouette (2025 rules)

Objective: in Mission 2 (Pirouette) of the 2025 METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition, the aircraft flies an accurate, fast circle in VTOL-QUAD (hover) mode.

Procedure:

- The aircraft takes off and climbs to 20 m above ground level.
- In VTOL-QUAD mode, the aircraft flies a circle of 15 m radius. Its heading points at the centre of the circle the whole time.
- Errors in altitude, radius and heading are penalised.
- A faster circle scores higher. Vertical take-off and landing time are not counted.

Score:

M2 = (1 / OEW2) × [0.3 / (|Δh|max + 1.5) + 1.2 / (|ΔR|max + 2) + 9 / (|Δψ|max + 15) + 18 / (Δt + 30)]

- |Δh|max: largest altitude error from 20 m.
- |ΔR|max: largest radius error from 15 m.
- |Δψ|max: largest heading error, in degrees.
- Δt: time to complete the circle, in seconds.
- OEW2: operating empty weight at the start of Mission 2, in kg.

What is scored in Mission 2: altitude, radius and heading accuracy, speed, and a low operating empty weight.

Source: https://vtol-ae.metu.edu.tr/en/rules.html

## Mission 3 of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition: Fixed-Wing Loiter Performance (2025 rules)

Objective: in Mission 3 (Fixed-Wing Loiter Performance) of the 2025 METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition, the aircraft flies figure-8 manoeuvres while carrying a slung load. It then drops the load on a target.

Procedure:

- The payload is a 0.5 kg water bottle on a 1 m rope, carried as a slung load. The aircraft enters the inspection area without the payload installed.
- The aircraft flies three figure-8 manoeuvres in the horizontal plane. The team chooses the circle radius. The organisers set the coordinates of point P.
- The aircraft then releases the payload onto a target with rings of 0.3 m, 0.6 m, 1.0 m, 1.5 m and 2.5 m radius.
- K_drop is set by the first contact point. It is 5 inside the innermost ring and falls linearly to 1 at the 2.5 m ring.
- The change in wingspan is scored, so teams are expected to design a reconfigurable wing.

Score:

M3 = (1 / OEW3) × (max(b1, b3) / min(b1, b3)) × (R_min / R + RMS_min / RMS + K_drop / 5)

- b1, b3: wingspan in Mission 1 and in Mission 3.
- R: average turn radius. R_min: smallest average turn radius of all teams.
- RMS: root-mean-square horizontal path error from the ideal figure-8. RMS_min: smallest RMS error of all teams.
- OEW3: operating empty weight at the start of Mission 3, in kg.

Source: https://vtol-ae.metu.edu.tr/en/rules.html

## Final scoring of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition (2025 rules)

The 2025 rules page of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition shows the final score formula as an image (images/final_score.png). The image reads:

Final Score = [Report Score] × (M1 + M2 + M3) × e_oew

e_oew = exp(−|OEW_est − OEW|² / 25)

OEW = max(OEW1, OEW2, OEW3)

How operating empty weight (OEW) enters:

- Each mission score is divided by the OEW measured at the start of that mission, in kg. A lighter aircraft scores higher.
- Teams must give an estimated OEW (OEW_est) in the final design report.
- The factor e_oew is 1 when the estimate equals the largest measured OEW. It gets smaller as the gap grows.
- OEW is the aircraft's basic weight with everything needed for operation, such as motors, batteries and the flight control computer. It does not include payload.

Other factors: the final design report score, out of 100 points, multiplies the sum of the three mission scores. Mission 1 adds laps and landing precision. Mission 2 adds hover accuracy and time. Mission 3 adds wingspan change, turn radius, path error and drop accuracy.

## Reports and deliverables of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition (2025)

Teams in the 2025 METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition handed in:

1. The application form (Word document), the online application, and a conceptual design report (the proposal). The conceptual report covers the design problem, the solution, the engineering reasoning, and how the configuration and subsystems were chosen.
2. A final design report and a proof-of-flight video.
3. Flight logs after each successful flight at the competition.

The organiser sets no page limit and gives no template for reports. The final design report content document scores the report out of 100 points:

| Section | Points | Main content |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Summary | 5 | Up to 2 pages. Selected design and key requirements |
| Management Summary | 5 | Team organisation, assignments, milestone chart |
| Conceptual Design | 10 | Requirements, concepts considered, selection process |
| Preliminary Design | 20 | Methods, sizing trades, lift, drag, propulsion, stability, performance estimates, estimated performance table |
| Detailed Design | 35 | Dimensions, structure, systems, weight and balance, flight and mission performance, 3-view drawings |
| Manufacturing | 10 | Processes, planned and actual milestones |
| Testing | 15 | Test plan, fail-safe scenario, checklists, results compared with predictions |

The final design report of the METU VTOL Aircraft Design Competition must also include the estimated operating empty weight.
